What Is AI?

AI is a tool, like a calculator or a spreadsheet. You do not need any technical background to use it.

The Intern Analogy

Think of AI as a well-read intern: fast, eager, and capable of great work — but it makes mistakes, needs clear instructions, and always requires you to review its output.

✍️
You Type
Instructions in plain English
⟶
⚙️
AI Processes
Predicts the best response
⟶
📄
You Get Output
Text you review & refine

You are always in control. AI generates — you decide.

How AI Works: LLMs in 60 Seconds

The tools we use today are called Large Language Models (LLMs).

📚
Trained on Text
Billions of pages of books, articles, and code
⟶
⚙️
Predicts Next Word
Like autocomplete, but vastly more sophisticated
⟶
📄
Generates Response
Word by word, building a coherent answer
Why This Matters

LLMs do not look up answers in a database. They predict text based on patterns learned during training. This is why they can be fluent and wrong at the same time — and why your review is always the final step.

Remember this term: LLM. We will come back to it later when we explore how AI is evolving.

What AI Can & Cannot Do

AI Does Well
  • Draft text and written content
  • Summarize long documents
  • Brainstorm ideas and options
  • Reformat and reorganize data
  • Explain concepts in simple terms
AI Cannot Do
  • Browse the internet in real time
  • Access your company's internal systems
  • Guarantee that its facts are correct
  • Make decisions or judgments for you
  • Replace your expertise and context
Key Takeaway

AI writes fast first drafts. Your job is to check the facts, adjust the tone, and add the context that only you know.

Your First Prompt

See the difference that adding structure makes to AI output.

Simple Prompt

What can you help me with at work?

Generic response — not specific to you

Structured Prompt

I work in [your department] at a manufacturing company. List 5 specific tasks you can help me with in my daily work. Keep each to one sentence.

Targeted, actionable suggestions

What Happens Next

The simple prompt returns generic advice. The structured prompt gives you targeted, department-specific suggestions. Same AI, same question — the difference is how you ask.

What to Trust, What to Verify

Read AI output like you would review a colleague's draft.

Generally Reliable
  • Identifying patterns and themes
  • Formatting and restructuring
  • Tone adjustment and rewording
  • Brainstorming and ideation
  • First drafts and outlines
Always Verify
  • Specific numbers and calculations
  • Dates, names, and citations
  • Causal claims and conclusions
  • Technical or regulatory accuracy
  • Anything you would not say yourself
Data Privacy

Follow your organization's data policy. When in doubt, don't paste anything you wouldn't email to a colleague.

LLM vs. Agentic AI

Two ways to work with AI — you have already seen both today.

💬 LLM / Chat Mode

  • • You type a prompt, AI responds
  • • One question at a time
  • • No memory between conversations
  • • AI cannot take actions or use tools
  • • Best for: drafts, brainstorming, Q&A
What you practiced today

🛠 Agentic AI

  • • AI plans multi-step workflows
  • • Reads files, writes files, runs tests
  • • Maintains context across sessions
  • • Uses tools: search, code execution, file I/O
  • • Best for: projects, codebases, research
Where AI is heading
Single prompt
Multi-turn chat
Multi-step project
Autonomous workflow
You Are Already Doing This

When you refined your prompt in the hands-on exercise, you were iterating — the first step toward agentic thinking. The next step is giving AI a project folder, an instruction file, and letting it work across files.

Q&A

Open Discussion Forum

"Is it safe to paste company data?"

Follow your organization's data policy. As a general rule: don't paste anything you wouldn't email to a colleague. Avoid personally identifiable information, financial account numbers, and classified project details unless your policy explicitly allows it.

"What if the output is wrong?"

AI output is always a draft, never a final answer. Read it critically. Check numbers against source data. Verify names and dates. If something sounds wrong, it might be — AI can present incorrect information confidently. Your review is always the final step.

"Can I use it for [my specific task]?"

Probably yes! The best way to find out is to try it. Use the 5 building blocks to write a clear prompt, and see what you get. If the first result isn't great, iterate — that's normal. The champion office hours are a great place to get help with specific use cases.

"Will AI replace my job?"

No. AI handles the tedious parts — first drafts, reformatting, brainstorming. You provide the judgment, expertise, and relationships that AI cannot replicate. Think of it as a tool that makes you faster, not a replacement.
