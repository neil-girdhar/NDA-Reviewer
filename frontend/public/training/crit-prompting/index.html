CRIT Prompting Framework

Use AI as your thought partner — not your search engine.

Most people use AI like a search engine: ask a question, get an answer. That dynamic makes AI the thought leader. CRIT flips the script: you are the thought leader, and AI becomes your thought partner.

Think of CRIT like cooking

A good meal depends on quality ingredients. CRIT is the set of ingredients that makes prompts produce reliable, usable output.

C Context (carbs) feeds understanding. R Role (fats) adds depth and credibility. I Interview (protein) pulls gaps out of your brain. T Task (seasoning) shapes the final deliverable.

The Four Ingredients

C Context

  • Brain dump what you already have: background, goals, constraints, stakeholders, and what you tried.
  • The richer and more specific the context, the better the output.
  • One inaccurate detail can ruin the result. Treat inaccuracies like toxic ingredients.

R Role

  • Assign AI a very specific expert identity: “You are a PhD behavioral economist specializing in …”.
  • Specificity increases trust and improves analysis depth.
  • Core principle: “We don’t hire a plumber to do our taxes.” Give the right expert for the job.

I Interview

  • Have the expert AI ask you questions — you provide the missing inputs.
  • This is what separates CRIT from a simple search query.
  • Core principle: human drives; AI guides.

T Task

  • Define the exact deliverable: format, length, audience, tone, and structure.
  • The task is the flavor; everything else exists to support it.
  • Example: “1-page executive-facing summary, bullet points, for a non-technical audience.”
Every CRIT prompt is a meal

Not every situation calls for a full meal. Sometimes a snack (just Context + Task) is enough. When quality matters, use all four ingredients.

Pro Tip: Use this Interview phrase

Ask me five questions, one at a time, in order to pull deeper context out of my head, to better achieve the stated task.

Example: from vague to usable

Without CRIT

Write me an email to my team about the new process.

With CRIT

C (Context): Team resists change. Rolling out new approval workflow Monday...

R (Role): You are an expert in change management and internal comms, with a PhD in psychology...

I (Interview): Ask me 5 questions, one at a time, to pull deeper context...

T (Task): 200-word team email, empathetic tone, 3 clear action items.

Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt

Research (including work discussed in “The Forklift Problem”) highlights a risk: if AI leads your thinking, some cognitive muscles atrophy. You feel productive, but you reduce your own growth.

Letting AI do your thinking is like hearing: “Lifting weights is great for your health” — then bringing a forklift to the gym. You get an outcome, but you avoid the work your brain needs.

CRIT keeps you lifting

AI spots the form while you do the thinking.

What's next

Pick a real task you do this week (an email, a summary, or an action plan). Write a first draft prompt using CRIT. Then iterate based on the output quality.

Start small: add Context, then add a clear Task. Upgrade to full CRIT when quality matters.
