Getting Started with AI

In 10 minutes, you will understand what AI is, meet Claude, and write your first prompt.

Beginner   No prerequisites   8-10 minute read

What Is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is software that can perform tasks that normally require human thinking. That includes understanding language, spotting patterns in data, making predictions, and generating text.

When you type a question into an AI tool and get a clear, structured answer back, that is AI at work. The software takes your input, processes it through a trained model, and produces an output that makes sense in context.

The Intern Analogy

Think of AI as a very well-read intern. It is fast, eager, and has absorbed a huge amount of information. It can draft documents, answer questions, and organize data much faster than you could alone. But like any intern, it needs clear instructions to do its best work. Give it vague directions and you get vague results. Give it specific, structured instructions and you get far better output.

And like an intern, it makes mistakes. Sometimes confidently. That is why you always review its work before using it.

What AI Is NOT

  • Not conscious. AI does not have feelings, awareness, or desires. When it says "I think," it is using a language pattern, not reporting an inner experience.
  • Not all-knowing. AI models learn from training data. They do not have access to everything ever written, and their knowledge has a cutoff date.
  • Not a replacement for judgment. AI is a tool. It can help you draft, analyze, and brainstorm, but final decisions should always involve a human who understands the context.
Limitation

AI can sound authoritative even when it is wrong. Confident-sounding language does not mean the content is accurate. Always verify important facts before acting on them.

Meet Claude

Claude is an AI assistant built by Anthropic. It reads your text input and generates written responses. You will use it to practice the concepts in this guide.

How to Access Claude

  1. Open your browser and go to claude.ai.
  2. Sign up with your email, or use Google or single sign-on (SSO) if your organization provides it.
  3. Confirm your email if prompted, then log in.
Using a Company Account

If your organization provides a Claude Team or Enterprise account, use that instead of creating a personal account. These plans offer stronger data privacy protections. Check with your IT department if you are unsure.

The Interface

Once logged in, you will see a simple chat interface:

  • Message box at the bottom — type your prompts here and press Enter to send.
  • Conversation area in the center — your messages and Claude's responses appear here.
  • Sidebar on the left — shows your conversation history. Click any past conversation to reopen it.
  • New Chat button — starts a fresh conversation. Each new conversation is independent.
Each Conversation Starts Fresh

Claude does not remember previous conversations. When you start a new chat, it has no knowledge of what you discussed before. This is by design. If you need to reference earlier work, paste the relevant context into your new conversation.

Your First Conversation

Copy and paste this into Claude's message box, or type something similar in your own words:

Hi Claude, I'm new to using AI assistants. Can you explain what kinds of tasks you can help me with at work? Please give me 5 examples with one sentence each.

Press Enter. Within a few seconds, Claude responds with a structured list. Your results will vary, but you can expect a clear, organized answer.

What to Notice

  • Claude responds quickly, usually within a few seconds.
  • The response follows the instructions you gave (five examples, one sentence each).
  • The tone is professional and direct.

Now Follow Up

This is a conversation, not a one-shot query. Try a follow-up message:

Can you expand on the third example? Give me a specific scenario where I might use that at work.

Claude remembers everything within the current conversation and builds on what came before. You can keep going back and forth to refine the answer.

Key Insight

Working with AI is iterative. Your first prompt is a starting point, not a final answer. The real value comes from the back-and-forth: send, read, adjust, repeat.

The Five Building Blocks of a Good Prompt

Every effective prompt draws from five building blocks. You do not need all five every time, but knowing them gives you a framework for writing prompts that consistently produce useful results.

Role

What it does: Tells the AI who to be. When you assign a role, you steer three things at once: the tone of the response, the depth of detail, and the vocabulary it uses.

Example:

You are a senior project manager with 15 years of experience.

Without a role, Claude defaults to a general-purpose voice. With a role, the output shifts to match. A "patient tutor" produces step-by-step explanations. A "senior analyst" produces concise, data-focused language.

Task

What it does: States exactly what you need done. A strong task uses a specific action verb and leaves no room for guessing.

Weak: "Help with my report."

Strong: "Summarize these meeting notes into 5 bullet points, each one sentence long."

Action verbs that work well: Summarize, list, compare, draft, rewrite, extract, analyze, explain, outline, translate.

If you only have time for one building block, make it this one.

Context

What it does: Provides the background information the AI needs to give a relevant answer. Without context, Claude fills in the gaps with assumptions that may not match your situation.

Without context: "Write a project update email."

With context: "Write a project update email. The project is a website redesign. The design phase finished on schedule, but backend development is delayed by 3 days because a team member is unavailable."

Context transforms generic output into something specific and useful.

Format

What it does: Tells the AI how to structure its output. Without format instructions, Claude chooses its own structure, which often defaults to paragraphs.

Common format specifications:

  • "Use bullet points"
  • "Create a table with columns for Name, Status, and Due Date"
  • "Write this as an email with a subject line, greeting, body, and sign-off"
  • "Organize under three headers: Summary, Risks, Next Steps"

Specifying format saves you the work of reformatting the output after the fact.

Constraints

What it does: Sets boundaries and rules for the output. Constraints tell Claude what to do and what not to do.

Types of constraints:

  • Length: "Keep it under 200 words"
  • Tone: "Use a formal, professional tone"
  • Inclusion: "Include at least one example for each point"
  • Exclusion: "Do not use technical jargon"
  • Scope: "Focus only on Q3 results"

Constraints prevent the AI from going off track. Without them, output can be too long, too technical, or include information you did not want.

Tips for Your First Week

Your first week with AI is about building comfort, not producing perfect output.

Start simple. Do not try to write a perfectly engineered prompt on day one. Ask a straightforward question. See what comes back. Adjust from there.

Tell Claude what to change. If the response is not what you wanted, you do not need to start over. Just say "Make it shorter," "Use bullet points instead," or "Focus more on the budget section." Claude adapts based on your feedback within the conversation.

You cannot break anything. There is no wrong prompt. Claude does not save your mistakes, judge your questions, or lock you out for experimenting. Type freely and learn by doing.

Be specific when it matters. Compare "Write a summary" to "Write a 3-paragraph summary of this report for my VP, focusing on risks and next steps." The second prompt gives Claude much more to work with.

Your First Week

Think of your first week as practice. The goal is not perfect output — it is getting comfortable typing to an AI and seeing what comes back. Your prompting skills develop through use, not study.

Common Questions

Does Claude remember previous conversations?

No. Each conversation starts fresh. Claude has no memory of past chats. If you need context from a previous conversation, copy and paste the relevant parts into your new chat.

Can Claude access the internet?

No. Claude works from its training data — the large collection of text it learned from during initial training. It cannot browse websites, check current prices, or retrieve live information. For anything that requires up-to-the-minute data, use a primary source.

Is my conversation private?

It depends on your plan. Free-tier conversations may be used to improve the model. Team and Enterprise plans offer stronger privacy protections. Check with your IT department for details on your organization's plan.

What if Claude gives a wrong answer?

That is normal. AI models sometimes produce inaccurate information — a behavior called hallucination, where the model generates plausible-sounding but incorrect content. Do not treat AI output as guaranteed fact. Always verify important claims, especially numbers, dates, and technical details.

Do I need to be polite to Claude?

No. Claude is software, not a person. Politeness does not affect the quality of the output. That said, writing clear, direct instructions is the best way to get good results. Write prompts the same way you would write a clear work email: direct, specific, and structured.
